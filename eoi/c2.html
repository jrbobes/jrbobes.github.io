💡 Learning Guide

🎯 British English Pronunciation Tips

'R' Sounds: In British English, 'r' is only pronounced at the beginning of words or before vowels (e.g., "car" vs "car park").
Vowel Shifts: Practice the differences between /ɑː/ (bath) and /æ/ (trap), and /ɔː/ (thought) vs /ʌ/ (strut).
Stress and Intonation: English uses stress patterns to convey meaning. "REcord" vs "reCORD" changes the word's function.
Connected Speech: Native speakers link words together. Practice "gonna" (going to), "wanna" (want to), and other reductions.
Rhotic Features: British English speakers may use linking 'r' between words: "idea of" sounds like "idea_rof".

📚 C1/C2 Grammar Focus

Advanced Conditionals: Master mixed and inversion conditionals for sophisticated argument construction.
Passive Structures: Use passive voice to shift focus and create more formal, academic tone.
Nominalisation: Convert verbs to nouns for academic writing: "The implementation of..." instead of "We implemented..."
Cleft Sentences: Use "It is/was... that" to emphasise information: "It was her dedication that made the difference."
Subjunctive Mood: Use subjunctive in formal contexts: "I suggest that he be informed" or "It is essential that she arrive on time."

🎓 C2 Vocabulary Building

Collocations: Learn word pairs that naturally go together: "profound impact," "widespread concern," "heated debate."
Synonyms with Nuance: Understand subtle differences between similar words: "criticise" vs "censure" vs "condemn."
Register Awareness: Know when to use formal ("utilise"), standard ("use"), or informal ("use up") vocabulary.
Idiomatic Expressions: Master idioms relevant to professional contexts: "think outside the box," "go the extra mile."
Academic Discourse Markers: Use: "Whereas," "Furthermore," "Conversely," "To elaborate," "In essence."
